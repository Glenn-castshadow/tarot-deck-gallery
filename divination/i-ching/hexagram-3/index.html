I Ching · Emergence

Hexagram 3: Zhūn 屯

Seedling breaking through soil

Trigrams
Thunder below, Water above
Keyword
Emergence

What hexagram 3 means

Beneath a covering of Water, Thunder stirs and pushes upward like a seedling in damp ground. Early growth can be slow and tangled without that meaning it will fail.

A question to sit with

What early stage deserves patience rather than a verdict?

The six lines, bottom to top

A changing line is read on its own. These are original reflections written for this site, not a translation.

Line 1 · The beginning

At the very start, hesitation can be a sensible response to tangled ground. Staying steady in your intention while gathering helpers gives an early effort somewhere firm to stand. Who could you ask to share the first stretch with you?

Line 2 · The inner position

A cart standing still with its horse unhitched pictures a start that keeps stalling. An offer that looks like an intrusion may turn out to be something else, and there is no need to accept or refuse in haste. Waiting for clarity is a legitimate choice at this central point.

Line 3 · The threshold

Chasing a deer into the forest without a guide leads deeper into unfamiliar thickets. If you are at a threshold without the knowledge you need, stopping is a form of wisdom rather than defeat. Consider what guidance would make the next move a sound one.

Line 4 · Entering the outer

Once more the horse and cart stand apart, and this time the line points towards finding a companion for the road. Asking for help is fitting when you are stepping into a wider sphere. Where could a partnership ease what you cannot move alone?

Line 5 · The position of authority

Even with real influence, you may find generosity blocked by early conditions. Small, patient gestures may accomplish more here than a large push that the moment cannot hold. What modest step is possible even if the grand one is not?

Line 6 · The end, and excess

The horse and cart part again, and the image is one of weeping. When difficulties seem endless after a hard beginning, it may help to step back rather than keep straining. Grief over a stalled start is understandable, and it need not be the last word.
