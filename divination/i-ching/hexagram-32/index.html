I Ching · Constancy

Hexagram 32: Héng 恆

Wind and thunder, steady

Trigrams
Wind below, Thunder above
Keyword
Constancy

What hexagram 32 means

Wind and Thunder move continuously, yet their pairing here describes something durable rather than erratic. What lasts is rarely still; it renews its own motion in a consistent direction.

A question to sit with

What steady, renewing motion has kept this going so far?

The six lines, bottom to top

A changing line is read on its own. These are original reflections written for this site, not a translation.

Line 1 · The beginning

Seeking permanence too quickly and too deeply puts strain on what is still forming. Where you want something to last, let it grow at its own pace. Early insistence can undermine durability.

Line 2 · The inner position

Unease lifts as effort comes to fit capacity. Consider where a moderate, sustainable pace would suit you better than an intense one. Proportion is a quiet form of constancy.

Line 3 · The threshold

Inconsistency of character leaves others unsure what to expect. If conduct shifts with every mood, notice the cost to trust. Steadiness of intention is what makes a person reliable.

Line 4 · Entering the outer

Hunting in a field with no game describes persistence in the wrong place. At the entry to the outer world, consider whether your effort is aimed where results are possible. Constancy works only when directed well.

Line 5 · The position of authority

Steady adherence can suit a supporting role while a leading one may call for flexibility. Where you hold firm, ask whether the situation needs loyalty or adaptation. Constancy is not the same as rigidity.

Line 6 · The end, and excess

Constant stirring that never settles marks the top of this figure. Where you notice yourself always stirring, a pause may do more than further effort. Lasting things need stillness as well as motion.
