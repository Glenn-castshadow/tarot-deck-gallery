I Ching · Dispersal

Hexagram 59: Huàn 渙

Wind scattering standing water

Trigrams
Water below, Wind above
Keyword
Dispersal

What hexagram 59 means

Wind moves across Water and scatters what had gathered into a single mass. Something rigid can be softened and redistributed this way, but the pieces still need somewhere useful to land.

A question to sit with

Where do the scattered pieces of this need somewhere useful to land?

The six lines, bottom to top

A changing line is read on its own. These are original reflections written for this site, not a translation.

Line 1 · The beginning

The image is help from a strong horse, arriving early. When scattering first begins, prompt support can restore order before the pieces drift too far apart.

Line 2 · The inner position

The image is a low table within reach, something solid to rest against while things scatter. The support to lean on may be close to home.

Line 3 · The threshold

Letting a narrow self-concern dissolve can free you for something larger. Putting your own interests aside for a while may be the wisest form of dispersal.

Line 4 · Entering the outer

Breaking up a close-knit faction can open the way to a wider gathering, like scattered stones forming a hill. As you enter the outer world, notice which small loyalties might give way to a broader common purpose.

Line 5 · The position of authority

From the place of authority, a clear call can spread through a scattered group the way warmth spreads through the body. Sharing out what has been held in reserve can draw people together again.

Line 6 · The end, and excess

Stepping well clear of a situation that is doing damage is the image here. Where dispersal is the wise course, distance itself can be a form of protection.
