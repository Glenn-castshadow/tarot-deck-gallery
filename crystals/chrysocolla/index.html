What Chrysocolla means

Blue-green with the softness of shallow lagoon water, chrysocolla forms where copper ore has weathered and slowly changed its colour underground. It speaks in a quieter register than turquoise, often found beside it, asking for a softer voice rather than a bold one.

Emotional properties

Escalation rarely wins the room it's aimed at. Set chrysocolla on the table between you when a conversation keeps climbing past what anyone meant; its blue-green is the colour of water too shallow to shout across. Say the last word at half the volume of the first.

Spiritual properties

Chrysocolla's name is Greek for gold-glue, after the copper minerals ancient goldsmiths used to solder their work. In meditation it asks what you have been holding together by keeping quiet, and whether the join would hold if you spoke. A seam that shows is still a seam that holds.

Physical properties

Soft and faintly chalky, chrysocolla feels more like dry clay than stone, and a thumb moving over it slows down of its own accord. Rub it while you rehearse something you need to say, one slow pass per sentence, and your speech takes the stone's pace. It is a copper mineral, so wash your hands afterwards.

Care and cleansing

Copper runs through chrysocolla, so never use it in an elixir and wash your hands after handling it. Its soft surface dulls in a soak, so keep it out of water and cleanse it with a dry cloth or smoke instead. Charge it in gentle moonlight, and give it a padded box away from the rest of the collection.
