What Jet means

Millions of years of pressure turned this ancient wood into something hard enough to carve, and jet still remembers its origin: light in the hand, warm against the skin, nothing like true stone at all. It argues that something organic can still be trusted as protection.

Emotional properties

In the days after a loss, you do not need anything that sparkles or asks you to feel better. Jet asks nothing of you: plain black, light in the pocket, company rather than a fix. Polished, it gives back a soft, deep shine instead of a flash. Grief can sit next to it without being asked to perform.

Spiritual properties

Whitby jet, cut from a stretch of the Yorkshire coast, dressed a whole era of Victorian mourning, worn as a public sign of a private loss. Grief allowed to be seen, rather than tidied away before it is ready, is what this stone actually asks for. Rubbed briskly against wool, it holds a static charge, the same old trick amber performs, a small spark from a stone that started as a tree. Old sorrow, given the chance, still carries its own charge.

Physical properties

Being organic rather than mineral, jet warms in the hand almost as fast as amber does, closer to wood than to stone once you have held it a minute. Its lightness surprises anyone expecting the weight of true stone, since fossilised wood keeps something of the original tree's lack of density. Carry it through a day that needs comfort more than protection, and let that quick warmth be the whole of the reassurance.

Care and cleansing

Soft enough to scratch or crack under a hard knock, jet is best kept away from harder stones and out of any ultrasonic cleaner. A soft, slightly damp cloth is all the cleaning it needs, and a few minutes in a closed hand charges it.
