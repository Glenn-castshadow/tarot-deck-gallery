What Petrified wood means

Silica crept into this tree cell by cell long after it fell, filling and replacing the dead tissue while keeping the original grain intact. Whole forests fossilised this way lie in Arizona, logs over two hundred million years old that have cracked into rounds as neat as sawn timber.

Emotional properties

Years pass and change nearly everything about a person except some stubborn, recognisable core that keeps showing through. That kind of continuity is easy to doubt in the middle of a hard stretch, when nothing about you seems to be holding steady. Petrified wood argues otherwise just by existing, proof that a shape can survive a change total enough to leave none of the original material behind. The grain outlasts the tree that grew it, the same way that core outlasts every version of you it has survived.

Spiritual properties

No person ever saw this tree standing, yet the stone still carries the pattern of its growth. Much of what a family hands down is just as quiet, a recipe, a saying, a way of folding sheets, kept alive only because someone went on doing it. Somewhere a card in a grandmother's handwriting is still taped inside a cupboard door.

Physical properties

Weight is the surprise in petrified wood: bark and grain still visible on the surface, while the stone underneath sits far heavier than a piece of true timber that size. That weight suits the soles of bare feet at home, through the small waits where standing still is the whole task, a kettle coming to the boil, a stretch held to the count. The mismatch between the look of wood and the weight of stone is exactly the point, ground disguised as memory.

Care and cleansing

Nothing much harms petrified wood, since quartz has taken the place of the timber, so an old toothbrush dipped in water will lift dust out of its grain. Charge it outdoors overnight in the garden, and give it an open shelf rather than a crowded box, where its weight could crack softer stones.

Who would still recognise the piece of you that time has never actually touched?
