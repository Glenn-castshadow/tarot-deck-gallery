What Turquoise means

Rainwater carrying copper down through cracked desert rock meets aluminium and phosphate, and turquoise settles out in veins of blue-green. Egyptians mined it in Sinai, Persians near Nishapur and Ancestral Puebloans across the American Southwest, an ocean away from both.

Emotional properties

Pack turquoise for a departure that unsettles you more than you have said out loud, a move, a flight, a first day somewhere unfamiliar. It will not take the nerves away, but it gives them something small and blue to sit beside. Keep it in the pocket you pat for your keys, so every check for the keys finds the stone as well.

Spiritual properties

Aztec craftsmen set thousands of small turquoise pieces into masks and ornaments, so that a single face was built from countless fragments of blue. A voice gets built the same way, one small true sentence at a time, and the throat learns by practice more than by any single brave speech. Say one plain, true thing each day this week and watch the mosaic start to form.

Physical properties

Old turquoise jewellery often carries a faint sheen exactly where a thumb has rubbed it for years, worn smoother by the same small motion each time. Wear a piece where your skin meets it often, a ring or a bracelet rather than a drawer, and let that daily contact settle into an easy, familiar rhythm. The comfort comes from habit, the same small touch returned to many times a day.

Care and cleansing

Porous, and often stabilised or dyed, turquoise soaks up water, perfume and skin oils, which can shift its colour, and it is a copper mineral, so keep it from elixirs and wash your hands after handling. Put it on after hand cream, wipe it dry when it comes off, charge it in your hands, and store it in a pouch.
