What Pearl means

A pearl is thin layers of nacre, mostly aragonite, laid down inside a living mollusc around a parasite or scrap of tissue, rarely a grain of sand. Cleopatra, Pliny wrote, dissolved one in vinegar to win a wager with Mark Antony.

Emotional properties

A strand passed down through a family comes with more than its pearls: the wedding it was worn to, the drawer it waited in, the last person who fastened it. Wearing it keeps that tenderness close without asking you to live in the past. The clasp has been mended twice, and nobody minds.

Spiritual properties

In the tarot's Moon card, a crayfish climbs out of a pool onto a long path, the old image of what rises from the deep mind. Pearl comes from the same dark water, and it keeps company with dreams, hunches and half-memories that surface slowly and seldom in words. Keep a notebook by the bed this week and write down whatever rises before it sinks again.

Physical properties

Against the edge of a tooth a real pearl feels faintly gritty, the fine overlapping edges of its nacre, where a plastic bead slides smooth. Roll one slowly across the back of the hand in the quiet minutes before sleep, as part of setting the day down. A few slow passes are enough, a rhythm rather than a rub.

Care and cleansing

Perfume, hairspray, cosmetics and sweat all carry acids that dull or pit pearl's soft nacre, so put it on last and take it off first. Draw a damp chamois over it after each wearing, let moonlight charge it, and lay it in a lined pouch, any strand flat so the silk does not stretch.
