What Chrysoprase means

Warmth does not always look bright; chrysoprase carries it in a pale apple green instead, coloured by traces of nickel. Its name borrows Greek words for gold and for leek, an odd pairing that has stuck for centuries.

Emotional properties

You already know the particular tiredness of scrolling past other people's version of enough, and feeling a little further behind for having looked. Chrysoprase does not argue you out of wanting more; it just asks what you already have that you have stopped noticing. Held for a quiet minute, its green tips the balance from wanting toward having.

Spiritual properties

Bring gratitude to chrysoprase as a discipline rather than a mood, something returned to on the days it feels hardest to access rather than only the easy ones. Silesian mines in what is now Poland worked it for centuries, long before nickel was understood as the source of its colour. Count three things before sleep, with the stone beside the list.

Physical properties

Chrysoprase has the waxy, close-grained feel of all chalcedony, and its apple green is the brightest thing on a grey desk. On a day that has shrunk to not enough time and not enough of you, turn it over once for each small job finished. By evening it has kept a count you would never have kept yourself.

Care and cleansing

Chrysoprase can pale in strong sun and heat, so charge it in an hour of morning light rather than on a sill. Wrap it in a cloth between uses to keep the light off. Being chalcedony, it takes a plain rinse under the tap without any fuss.

A question to sit with

How much do you already have that you have simply stopped noticing?
