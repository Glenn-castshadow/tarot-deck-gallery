What Cavansite means

Cavansite takes its name from its own chemistry, calcium and vanadium and silicate compressed into one blue word. It forms in small blue rosettes inside basalt, rare enough that most people who work with stone never hold one.

Emotional properties

Turn a cavansite specimen under a desk lamp on a flat, grey afternoon, and its blue answers back brighter than anything else in the room. Flatness lies about how much is left to be surprised by. That blue does not wait for a special occasion, and neither does the part of you that can still be caught off guard.

Spiritual properties

Vanadium is the metal behind cavansite's blue, and it takes its own name from Vanadis, an old name for the Norse goddess Freyja, chosen for the beauty of the compounds this metal makes. Sitting with a colour that owes its name to a goddess of beauty is reason enough to look closely at what is actually in front of you, rather than rushing past it. The blue does not need the myth to be beautiful, but it is nice that the myth agrees.

Physical properties

Tiny is the honest word for cavansite's crystals, barely the size of a grain of rice and clustered into rosettes far too delicate for a pocket or a bag. Set a small specimen on a windowsill instead, somewhere it can be looked at rather than carried; noticing it is the practice, not handling it. A closer look, not a longer hold, is what this stone is built for.

Care and cleansing

Cavansite's crystals are small and easily knocked loose, so handle a specimen by its base, not its points. Cleanse it with a light brush rather than a rinse, since water can loosen crystals in their matrix. Charge it near a window in indirect light, and store it upright, away from anything that could jostle it.
