What Alexandrite means

Alexandrite changes colour with the light around it, green by day and plum-red by lamplight, as if it refuses to be pinned to one mood. Keeping it near reminds you that changing your mind is not the same as losing yourself.

Emotional properties

Alexandrite holds two feelings side by side without making one win, relief and loss, excitement and caution. A new job, a move, an ending that opens into something else: none of it announces itself in advance. Slip the stone on before a decision that keeps flipping in your mind, and let its own flip remind you that indecision is not a flaw. Your first instinct and your second thoughts can both be right.

Spiritual properties

Named for the young heir who would become Czar Alexander II, after its discovery in the emerald mines of the Urals, this stone still carries a sense of a threshold crossed. Sit with it in low light and watch the colour turn, and let that shift stand in for whatever change in yourself still feels unfinished. Its lesson is that transformation is rarely one leap; it is a slow series of angles you tilt toward. Becoming someone new happens the same way, not all at once.

Physical properties

Chrysoberyl takes a high polish and feels dense for its size, real weight held in a small stone. That density steadies you when nerves run high before something new begins. Handled often, it warms quickly and holds that warmth a while, like a hand you keep returning to. Its weight alone, resting in your palm, can be enough to still a fidgeting hand.

Care and cleansing

Alexandrite is hard and tough, so a rinse under running water and a soft cloth are all it needs. Charge it by a window and again under a lamp, so it spends time in both of its colours. It is harder than most stones you own, so store it where it cannot scratch them.

A question to sit with

What would change if you let two conflicting feelings be true at the same time?
